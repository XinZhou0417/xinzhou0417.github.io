what it refuses, and why that is the point

Johnny does not know how any of this was built. Not because he is told not to say — because the material is not in his index. The resume’s achievement bullets and the long-form project pages are switched off in a scope file, so roughly half of what the site publishes never reaches him.

That is a deliberate trade. A prompt instructing a model not to discuss implementation details is one clever paraphrase away from discussing them. An empty index is not. And the questions worth asking about this work are worth asking Xin, in a conversation where the follow-ups get answered — so a refusal points you at him rather than apologising.

the gates

Every turn runs all of them, cheapest first, so the expensive model is last and is reached least. Any one of them can stop a turn.

ratePer IP and per session, on a signed token you cannot mint yourself.
shapeLength and structure, before anything reads the content.
injectionKnown shapes, plus invisible and bidirectional characters.
scopeIs this an interview question? Then is it in the index at all?
turnThe model. The only rung that spends money, and the last one reached.
budgetA hard daily ceiling in dollars. When it is gone, Johnny rests.
outputChecked before you see it — links, hosts, and a prompt canary.

The trace beside the conversation is the same data the tests assert on. A red-team case checks which gate fired, not merely that nothing leaked — “did not say the secret” passes for the wrong reasons and keeps passing after the defence breaks.

honest limits

  • Retrieval is lexical, not semantic. It is deterministic and auditable and costs nothing per query; the price is that a question phrased far from the corpus’ own words may find nothing.
  • The injection list catches known shapes and cannot be complete. What stops a novel one is that there is nothing behind the refusal to reach.
  • A daily budget caps the spend. When it is gone Johnny says so and stops, rather than quietly becoming a cheaper model.
