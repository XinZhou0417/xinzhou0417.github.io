Privacy

Last updated 27 August 2026

This page explains what xzhou.au collects, why it collects it, how long it keeps it, and who else can see it. It describes what the code actually does.

Contact for anything on this page: xinzhou0417@gmail.com.

## Reading the site

The site is static, served from GitHub Pages. It does not use cookies, analytics, tracking pixels, or anything from a third party. There is no advertising, and nothing here is sold or shared with anyone.

## Asking the assistant a question

When you send a question, the following is written to a database on my own server in Australia, as one row:

  • the question and the answer,
  • which of the safety checks ran and what each decided,
  • how many tokens the answer cost and which model wrote it,
  • and a keyed hash of your IP address, and of a few ordinary browser characteristics, never the values themselves. That lets me count repeated use without identifying anyone.

The browser characteristics are the ones any page can already read: which browser you are using, your language and time zone, your screen size, and how much memory and how many processor cores the device reports. They are hashed together with your IP address into one value that identifies a budget, not a person. It is never shown to anyone, never linked to an account, and never used to decide what you may read. Its only job is to stop one visitor using up the day's allowance and then starting again, which would leave nothing for anybody else.

There is no canvas or WebGL fingerprinting. That is the technique that can pick one person out of millions. This site does not need it and does not use it.

Everything is deleted after 14 days. The code enforces that window and a test checks it.

The transcript you can see in the chat window is kept only in your own browser, for that tab, and disappears when you close it.

Before your first question the site runs Cloudflare Turnstile, a check that you are a person and not a script. Cloudflare handles that and I do not receive anything from it beyond pass or fail.

Please do not type anything into the box that you would mind me reading, because it is a free-text field on a public page and I do read them. That is how I find out where the assistant answers badly.

## Talking to it

You can speak to the assistant instead of typing. Nothing listens until you ask it to, with /unmute or the key in the title bar, and your browser asks for the microphone before anything opens.

While a call is open, your audio goes to AssemblyAI, which turns it into text, and the assistant's replies go to Murf.ai, which turns them into speech. LiveKit carries the audio between your browser and my server. Each is a company with its own privacy terms.

No audio is kept on either side, neither by me nor as a recording anywhere on my server.

What you said appears in the input line first. You read it, edit it if it came out wrong, and press enter yourself, so a spoken question is stored exactly like a typed one, under the 14 days above. Nothing you did not send is kept.

I also record how long the microphone was open and how many characters were spoken back. That is what a call costs to run, and it is how the day's allowance is counted. I do not record the words.

## Sending it a picture

You can attach a picture to a question. A second assistant looks at it on its own, with no memory of the conversation, and describes what it sees to the first.

  • The picture is never written to disk. It is held in the server's memory for ten minutes at most, then discarded, whether or not you send it.
  • It is never served back out. There is no address anywhere on this site that returns a picture somebody uploaded. The preview you see is your own browser showing you your own file.
  • The filename is thrown away and never read.
  • The row described above records how many pictures came with the question, never what was in them.

## Leaving me a message

The assistant can take a message for me and email it on. It is the one thing on this site that reaches me directly, so it is worth being exact about.

If you ask it to, it collects three things and nothing else: a name, a way for me to reply, and your message. It shows you the exact text that would be sent, word for word, before anything goes anywhere. Nothing is sent unless you say yes to that text.

  • It is sent to me by email, through Resend, as plain text. Your contact goes in the reply-to line so I can answer you.
  • Nothing is stored. The draft lives in the server's memory for ten minutes and is discarded the moment it is sent or the moment it expires. It is never written to the database that holds questions, and no copy is kept anywhere but my inbox.
  • Only text. There is no way to attach a picture or a file to it, and no way for the assistant to send it to anyone but me.
  • One message per conversation, and a few per day from one connection. That stops it being used to send mail through my address.

The email in my inbox is the only copy. If you want it deleted, say so and I will delete it.

## Subscribing

If you subscribe, Stripe collects and holds your payment details. I never see them. Stripe acts as merchant of record through Link. Its own privacy terms apply to the payment.

I store four things:

  • your email address, which Stripe gives me,
  • your Stripe customer reference,
  • your subscription's status and current period,
  • and a running total of what you have used this period.

Your email address is the whole of your account. There is no password and no profile. All four are kept for as long as you have a subscription. Ask me and I will delete it, and you can also ask Stripe directly, in which case Stripe deletes its record and tells my service to erase mine.

Signing in sends a six-digit code to your email address, through Resend. The email contains no links and nothing about it is tracked, so there is no record of opens or clicks. Resend handles the delivery and its own terms apply.

Being signed in sets one cookie, xz_ent. It shows that you are signed in and nothing else, holds no personal information, cannot be read by scripts, lasts thirty days, and is replaced each time it is used so that a copied cookie stops working. And signing out ends it immediately.

## Who else is involved

What they see
Anthropic the text of your question, and any picture attached to it, to write an answer. Nothing that identifies you goes with it
AssemblyAI your audio while you are speaking, to turn it into text
Murf.ai the text of the assistant's reply, to speak it aloud
LiveKit carries the audio of a call between your browser and my server
Cloudflare traffic to the site, and the human check
Stripe / Link everything about a payment, as merchant of record
Resend your email address and the sign-in code
GitHub serves the static pages

Each is a company with its own privacy terms. I use them because building any of it myself would be worse for you.

## Your rights

Ask me for a copy of what I hold about you, ask me to correct it, or ask me to delete it, and I will. Anything I hold is small enough to send you in an email.

If you think I have handled something badly, tell me first, because I would rather fix it. But you can also complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner.
